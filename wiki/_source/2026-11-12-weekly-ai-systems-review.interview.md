# Interview: Weekly AI systems review

Interviewed: 2026-11-12 · Idea: [[weekly-ai-systems-review]] · Capture: _source/2026-11-12-weekly-ai-systems-review.md

Interviewer's opening case against (stated before questions): a review loop for a system with no load yet mostly finds formatting drift; a recursive improver with nothing real to chew on rewrites its own instructions; a cross-project consistency layer becomes a second source of truth that drifts from the repos. Verdict given: good idea, wrong starting shape, ~60%.

**Q — The claim in one sentence? What does "improve everything as a whole" look like in a month, concretely enough that you'd know it happened?**
> The system continues to provide value to me. Saves me time, surfaces ideas, completes projects. Right now I am running in quick sand. Some projects and efforts work better than others. Hyrox is going great, Burrard Works is failing. So I would want the abillity to meausre the success of my projects and identify why some are stalled and why others are succeeding.

**Q — What triggered it? Was it Burrard Works specifically?**
> It was partly Burrard Works, but also learning how the Anthropic team operates. They released literature on this concept - every week you should be updating your system. So I want to mimic that.

**Q — Why do you believe it? Gut answer: what's different about how Hyrox runs versus Burrard Works?**
> Hyrox has a fixed date, and a program in place. It is also physical fitness and is going to happen. As we go through it, we are tweaking things, adding new features.
>
> Burrard Works is an idea, and the idea is messy. We have been going in circles for weeks and I am procrastinating. We dont have a hard deadline ( i blew it anyway). We also dont have any actual goal in place that I am being held accountable to. The big difference is that Hyrox has a cadence to it while Burrard works does not. So we always know what we are workign on next.

**Q — Strongest objection: a weekly review can detect that Burrard Works has no cadence, but it can't fix it. Burrard Works is stalled on an unmade decision about what the idea even is, and a review becomes one more meta-task to procrastinate on instead of making that call. Your answer?**
> My only hope is that we can surface this information consistently and design a habit around addressing it.

**Q — What would change your mind? What would you need to see after, say, four weekly reviews to decide this isn't worth it? And how does it sit with the monthly os-audit: above it, or one ritual replacing both?**
> If we saw that there was no progress made after a certain amount of time, it might be time to kill the project and be realistic. But this cannot happen, we need to get this launched yesterday. I don't know why I am procrastinating so hard on this it is unacceptable.
>
> I think the os-audit can consume it, the weekly review should check if things are moving and produces a journal of actions. Again, we should be able to consume as much information about how each project has changed. Not only in the movement towards its goal but in how the structure changed. What systems were updated. What scheduled tasks ?

**Q — Does it connect to existing pages?**
> Not asked: the wiki had no other pages at interview time.

**Q — Conviction: low, medium, or high?**
> High - I want this system to be self improving. I want to see us move things forward as a whole. And I want this all documented so we can see how we changed things.
